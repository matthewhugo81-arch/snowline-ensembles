Data sources, availability & chart guide

Read the ensemble

Each thin line is an individual ensemble member. The pink line is the arithmetic mean of the available members; the shaded band spans the 10th to 90th percentiles. It is an ensemble range, not a calibrated confidence interval. Missing values are gaps, never zeros.

Click or hover to inspect the same time in every panel. Drag horizontally to zoom all charts together. Use the time slider for keyboard inspection. Each panel also has its own optional minimum and maximum.

Right-click a chart or use its image button to Copy image, Save PNG or Save SVG. Images include the visible UTC window, location, model run information and cumulative precipitation context. If direct copying is unavailable, a PNG preview lets you hold or right-click the image to copy it, or save it.

Cumulative precipitation

The blue line uses the right-hand millimetre scale and sums the available-member ensemble mean for each complete period inside the visible window. It starts at zero at the first period boundary at or after the left edge; rain before that boundary and partial periods at the edges are excluded. The bottom readout states the exact counted UTC span, the total and member availability. Zooming or changing the forecast window recalculates the total. A missing mean stops the line and makes the full-window total unavailable. Compare means shows separate blue cumulative curves and totals for each model, distinguished by line style; forecasts are never blended.

Snow signal

The snow panel shows the share of available members forecasting at least 0.1 cm of snowfall in the selected accumulation period. This is model agreement, not a calibrated probability or a prediction of settling snow.

Source & timing

GFS, EC and GEM ensemble data: Open-Meteo Ensemble API. Model cards and each chart show the latest initialization reported by the feed, in UTC (00Z, 06Z, 12Z or 18Z), checked when the forecast loads. GEFS combines 0.25° and 0.5° data; if their runs differ, both are shown. Its upper-air temperatures use the 0.5° feed; precipitation, snowfall and mean sea level pressure can use both. These are rolling forecasts and may retain an older run at longer lead times. During an update, or when timing cannot be confirmed, the label says so. Retrieval time is shown separately.

Hourly values may be interpolated from coarser model output. Temperatures and mean sea level pressure (hPa) are instantaneous values sampled at the displayed times. Precipitation and snowfall are summed over complete preceding 1-, 3- or 6-hour windows; incomplete windows remain missing. Forecast availability differs by model and variable.

MOGREPS-G uses the free Met Office AWS dataset: 18 members, both pressure-level temperatures, precipitation, snowfall and mean sea level pressure from the same run, out to +246 hours. The latest complete run is checked every 3 hours. Publication is delayed after initialization; its actual run time is shown above. A new location can take around a minute to load, then benefits from caching.

MOGREPS is read on its native global grid, at 3-hour intervals. Six-hour totals combine two complete 3-hour periods; hourly totals are disabled when MOGREPS is displayed. Temperatures below the surface are masked. Snow water equivalent is converted to centimetres using a fixed 7:1 ratio, matching Open-Meteo’s snowfall convention; actual snow density varies. Comparisons align valid times, not initialization times.

The separate operational GFS line in the reference image is not included: this view uses ensemble members and their control only.
